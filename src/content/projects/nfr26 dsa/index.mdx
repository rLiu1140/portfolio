### Overview
The differential sprocket adapter (DSA) connects the differential to the drivetrain sprocket, transferring torque between the two. The differential shaft has a splined profile, while the drivetrain sprocket has six mounting holes.

I led this four-person project and took ownership of the design and manufacturing process, from initial modeling through FEA validation, optimization, CAM programming, and CNC machining.

### Requirements
1. Ensure concentricity between the differential shaft and sprocket.
2. Achieve a factor of safety (FoS) greater than 1.25 and a fatigue life beyond 400,000 cycles.
3. Minimize mass.
4. Successfully machine the part on the first attempt.

### Load Case
The load case was derived from the peak motor torque of 220 Nm, converted to an equivalent force of 4,800 N applied at each bolt hole.

During the second design iteration, an additional 2,290 N, opposing the direction of motion, was applied to represent the sprocket's reaction force on the inserts. This was calculated by scaling the bolt-hole force by the ratio of sprocket width to DSA width.

### Stock
- 7075-T6 aluminum disc, 1.6 in thick × 8.0 in OD.
- Spline profile cut concentrically via outsourced wire EDM.

### Design Iterations
#### V1

  <Image src={DSAv1Top} alt="DSA v1 Top" className="w-full h-auto rounded-md" />
  <Image src={DSAv1Bottom} alt="DSA v1 Bottom" className="w-full h-auto rounded-md" />

- 3-spoke design to minimize material and mounting hardware.
- Pocketed geometry, optimized and verified with FEA.
- Circularly arranged tabs for concentric mounting.
- Filleted edges to reduce stress concentrations.
- Chamfered edges to remove burrs and prevent mounting interference.

#### V2

<div className="grid grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2 gap-4">
  <Image src={DSAv2Top} alt="DSA v2 Top" className="w-full h-auto rounded-md" />
  <Image src={DSAv2Bottom} alt="DSA v2 Bottom" className="w-full h-auto rounded-md" />
</div>

- Added hole inserts for 1/4-28 bolts, reducing mass by downsizing from M10 fasteners.
- Increased spline cylinder diameter and plate thickness to reduce deflection and improve machinability.
- Updated chamfer and fillet dimensions.
- Switched the FEA load case from torque to force for more realistic loading.
- Added an opposing force at the hole inserts to represent the sprocket reaction load.

|---|---|---|
| Min. FoS | 2.625 | 4.8 |
| Fatigue life | 696,100 cycles | Passed (>1M cycles) |
| Fasteners | 3× M10 hex screws + locknuts | 3× 1/4-28 hex screws + locknuts |
| Part mass | 78.58 g | 97.14 g |
| Assembled mass | 180.58 g | 130.14 g (-27.94%) |

#### V3

<div className="grid grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2 gap-4">
  <Image src={DSAv3Top} alt="DSA v3 Top" className="w-full h-auto rounded-md" />

- Replaced the small concentricity tabs with a single large circular feature.
- Removed the integrated bosses in favor of separately machined spacers.

Together, these changes eliminated the need for rotational alignment between the two CNC setups and prevented feature breakage during machining. The assembly's function and weight were largely unchanged.

### Manufacturing
The DSA was machined in two setups on a Haas VF-2 CNC mill. A 4-jaw chuck with parallels and a dial indicator leveled the part, while an edge finder set the origin. Each setup began with a roughing pass to remove bulk stock, followed by finishing passes for the respective top and bottom surface features.

<Image src={DSABottom} alt="DSA Bottom" className="rounded-md max-w-full h-auto" />

The final part was finished with WD-40 and a Scotch-Brite pad to deburr the edges and produce a brushed appearance. The three spacers were turned separately and hand-fitted into the sprocket until a tight fit was achieved.

#### Challenges
The oversized round stock significantly increased manufacturing time, as it was difficult to seat squarely and securely in the chuck jaws.

An initial pass with a 1-in-diameter end mill lifted the stock, requiring the setup to be redone with a 1/2-in end mill. Reducing the stock size beforehand with a band saw was considered but avoided, as it would have added an extra manufacturing step.

### Results
The DSA integrated seamlessly into the drivetrain assembly and met all design requirements, successfully transferring torque from the differential shaft to the sprocket. The team's car completed the endurance event for the first time in a decade.
