### Overview
Formula SAE requires all teams to develop a mechanical steering system. Previously, Northwestern Formula Racing has used 4130 steel columns with welded connections to achieve a highly reliable and stiff system. However, reducing the weight of the steering system has been an area of interest.

I am independently working on a new steering system, exploring alternative materials and structural components to meet the design goals.

### Column Material Selection
#### Material Comparison
| Material | Shear Modulus (GPa) | Density (g/cm³) | G/ρ Ratio | Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4130 Steel** | 80 | 7.85 | 10.19 | Isotropic |
| 6061 Aluminum | 26 | 2.70 | 9.63 | Isotropic |
| 7075 Aluminum | 26.9 | 2.81 | 9.57 | Isotropic |
| **±45° CF Layup** | 20–30 | 1.50–1.60 | ~16.13 | Anisotropic |

- 4130 steel beats both aluminum alloys, but carbon fiber is the best on paper. However, bonded connections require a mechanical backup per the rules.

#### U-Joint Attachment Options
#### 4130 Steel
| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Steel w/ steel adapter + weld *(current)* | Most rigid | Heaviest, weld failure risk |
| Mil-Spec w/ adapter + transverse bolt/cross pins | Light, rigid, ~100 g lighter than current | Stress concentrations, wear? |

#### ±45° CF Column
| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Mil-Spec w/ adapter + epoxy + transverse bolt/cross pins | Lightest: ~100 g lighter joints + ~70 g lighter columns = **~170 g saved** vs. current | Stress concentrations, bond failure risk |

#### Takeaways
- 4130 steel has better torsional rigidity-to-weight than 6061 and 7075 aluminum.
- Mil-Spec U-joints are a low-cost weight-saving method.
    - Common in race cars; zero backlash is achievable.
- CF tubing minimizes weight but raises questions:
    - Bond reliability: Test and reference CF suspension arms.
    - Cross pins: How much does drilling into CF affect strength?
    - Weight savings: Is an additional 70 g worth it after saving 100 g from the U-joints?

### Carbon-Fiber Tube Selection
#### Manufacturer
Rock West Composites is one of the largest direct-to-consumer online suppliers of composite materials and is well known for supporting projects from universities and FSAE teams. Their website is transparent with engineering data, including exact ply layups and material properties, and they even provide calculators for mechanical analysis. They offer an extremely wide range of composite products, including various sizes of carbon fiber tubing specifically designed for torsional applications using ±45° layups.

#### Layup
±45° layups provide the highest strength and stiffness for carbon fiber tubes under torsion. This is because the tensile and compressive stresses generated during torsional loading are maximized at 45° to the tube axis. Rock West torsion tubes are specifically designed for torsional applications, with fibers oriented primarily at ±45° to maximize torsional performance.

#### Wall Thickness
According to FSAE rules, bonded joints within the steering system must have a mechanical backup, such as a bolt or pin. The primary failure modes around drilled holes under torsion are delamination due to stress concentration, matrix cracking, and pin/bolt tear-out. Using ⅛” walls significantly protects against these failures by decreasing shear stress, which is inversely proportional to wall thickness. The thicker wall also increases the bearing area between the fastener and the tube, which directly combats tear-out. Additionally, ⅛” walls allow for a reduced outer diameter while maintaining torsional stiffness, which helps with packaging at the cost of weight.
